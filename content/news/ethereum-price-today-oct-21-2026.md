Ethereum is trading around $2,420 on Wednesday 21 October, sitting just above the $2,400 zone buyers have protected over the past 48 hours. Ether reached highs above $2,700 earlier in the month, but sustained selling pressure has erased those gains. Bitcoin is hovering near $82,400, while Solana is changing hands around $154.

The prices in this paragraph come from market data compiled this morning. Crypto trades around the clock, so check the live chart further down for the latest figures.

## Spot Ether ETFs see sustained outflows

Institutional demand through US spot Ethereum ETFs remains weak. Ether funds saw roughly $320 million in net outflows last week, marking their third consecutive negative week.

Most of the redemptions continue to come from Grayscale's converted trust (ETHE), while inflows into lower-cost competitor funds have not been large enough to offset the exits. For context, bitcoin funds also recorded about $229 million in net outflows over the same period, showing that institutional investors are treading cautiously across the broader digital asset space.

## The ETH/BTC ratio sits near multi-year lows

One of the most discussed charts among market participants is the ETH/BTC pair, which measures ether's strength relative to bitcoin. 

The ratio has slipped toward 0.029, its lowest reading in over three years. Analysts point to several reasons for ether's lagging performance:

- **Institutional preference for bitcoin.** Spot bitcoin ETFs have absorbed the lion's share of institutional capital throughout 2026, reinforced by bitcoin's role as a macro hedge.
- **Layer 2 migration.** Much of Ethereum's transaction volume has shifted to low-cost Layer 2 networks. While this keeps user fees low, it has significantly reduced the amount of ETH burned on the main network, turning supply slightly inflationary in recent months.
- **No staking yield in US ETFs.** Unlike holding physical ether directly, US spot ether ETFs cannot stake their holdings, which makes them less attractive to yield-focused institutions.

## Macro pressure ahead of next week's Fed meeting

Macroeconomic uncertainty continues to cast a shadow over risk assets. The US Consumer Price Index published on 14 October showed headline inflation rising 0.6% in September and 3.7% year-on-year, driven mainly by a 20.4% surge in energy prices.

With inflation remaining well above the 2% target, expectations for immediate monetary loosening remain low. The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meets next week on 27 and 28 October. While markets expect the Fed to keep its benchmark interest rate steady in the 3.75% to 4% range, any hawkish commentary from Chairman Jerome Powell could test market sentiment.

## The price levels traders are watching

For ether, holding above $2,400 remains the immediate battle line. 

Underneath $2,400, technical analysts highlight the August swing low near $2,280 as the primary safety cushion, followed by the $2,150 zone. A break below those marks would signal a deeper correction.

On the upside, the 50-day moving average near $2,530 is the first hurdle buyers need to reclaim. Above that, resistance sits at the round $2,600 mark and the 200-day moving average around $2,760.

Technical momentum remains tepid. The daily Relative Strength Index (RSI) is hovering around 43, indicating mild selling pressure without touching oversold territory, while moving average convergence indicators remain flat.

## What to watch this week

- Whether ether holds $2,400 through the daily close or slides toward $2,280.
- Spot ETF flow figures toward the end of the week to see if outflows begin to taper.
- The ETH/BTC ratio for signs of bottoming out.
- US Treasury yields and crude oil prices in the lead-up to the 27 to 28 October Fed meeting.
