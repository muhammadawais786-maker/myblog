Crypto mining is the process some blockchains use to confirm transactions and add them to the shared record. In return for doing this work, miners earn newly created coins and the fees paid by users.

## How mining works

On a proof-of-work blockchain such as Bitcoin, miners gather pending transactions into a block. They then run powerful computers that guess numbers over and over until one produces a result that meets the network's difficulty target. The first miner to find it publishes the block, the other computers check it, and the winner collects the reward.

## Why it is done this way

The guessing takes real electricity and hardware. That cost is what protects the network. To rewrite history, an attacker would have to redo the work for every block after the one they wanted to change, while the honest miners keep adding new ones.

## What mining needs

- Specialised machines, called ASICs, for Bitcoin. Ordinary computers cannot compete.
- Cheap and reliable electricity, which is why mining farms cluster where power is inexpensive.
- Cooling and internet access.

## Mining and the environment

Proof-of-work mining uses a lot of energy, and this is a regular subject of debate. Some miners use surplus or renewable power. Other blockchains avoid the issue with a different system, [staking](/learn/what-is-staking/). Ethereum switched from mining to staking in 2022.

## Is it profitable?

It depends on the coin's price, the machines, the cost of power and how many other miners compete. Margins can disappear quickly when prices fall or when the reward is cut. Treat it as a business with real costs and risks, not a guaranteed income.
